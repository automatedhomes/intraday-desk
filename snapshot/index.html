How to read this

  • Probability of profit is how often this setup, traded in this direction, ended in a net profit on this stock, blended with the result across all stocks. Small samples are pulled toward the average, and the range shows the uncertainty (90%).
  • Expected profit is the average result per trade after charges. It ranks the list because a 40% win rate can still pay if wins are twice the size of losses.
  • Direction comes from the bias at the last close. Recheck it after 9:30 before acting on any trigger.
  • These are past results on a short window, not a forecast. When nothing has a positive expected profit, the best trade is no trade.

    Discipline rules

    1. Risk at most 1% of capital per trade; size from the stop, not from leverage.
    2. Stop for the day after 2 losing trades or a 2% loss.
    3. Only take setups that agree with the bias and VWAP side.
    4. No new entries after 2:30 PM; be flat by 3:15 PM (your broker auto-squares MIS a few minutes later, with a fee).
    5. Never move a stop further away or average a losing intraday trade.
    6. Prefer strategies whose backtest on this stock is positive after charges.

    Big money

    Loading NSE data…
    NSE publishes block deals (large trades in the 8:45-9:00 AM and 2:05-2:20 PM windows) and bulk deals (one client trading 0.5% or more of a company's shares in a day) with the client's name, quantity and price, plus FII/DII totals for the whole market. It does not publish the exact time of these trades, or which institution is behind any single trade on the screen. The last table fills that gap the only way possible: it shows the exact 5-minute candles where volume was many times normal, without the name of who traded.
